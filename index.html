Abstract

Humans constantly prioritize sensory information to make rapid, context-sensitive decisions, such as reacting to a sudden sound while focused on a visual task. Emulating this kind of adaptive, multimodal prioritization remains a fundamental challenge in artificial systems. In this work, we introduce a context-aware dynamic fusion approach that enables neural models to adaptively weight audio and visual modalities based on learned task relevance for egocentric gaze anticipation. Our method employs a lightweight policy network that governs the fusion of modality-specific embeddings, operating within a spatial-temporal separable framework to model both in-frame correlations and cross-frame attention shifts. Theoretically grounded in information theory and meta-learning principles, our approach learns to maximize mutual information between the fused representation and target gaze locations across varying contexts. Through extensive ablation studies and visualizations, we demonstrate that our model dynamically shifts modality weights in response to changing environmental conditions, providing insights into how machines can better allocate attention across sensory inputs. These findings have significant implications for designing more responsive, human-aligned systems in extended reality and assistive technology applications.
